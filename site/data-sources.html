Data sources

North Country Trail — full trail viewer, North Dakota to Vermont. Every layer: references + interpretation method.

The standing rule. Every layer below lists BOTH its references (service URL, pull date, query) AND its interpretation method — the exact rules used to turn raw data into the published classification — so that someone reading this page later can reproduce every classification from the references plus the stated rules, with nothing resting on undocumented judgment. Where a call is judgment rather than a published rule, it is labeled as judgment. Where a gap is known, it is stated as a gap, not filled in.

Freshness. The layers on this map are snapshots, pulled on the dates given. A pull date is a record of its date, not a live feed — the pull date on each layer is the freshness signal. No one at NCTA or the National Park Service was contacted for this viewer; everything here comes from public sources. The full per-step build notes live in the repository at site/data/SOURCES-step2.md, SOURCES-step3.md, SOURCES-step4.md, and SOURCES-step5.md; this page is the readable summary of those files, plus the cross-checks run when the layers were wired in.

The trail itself — base line and chapters

Base line. North Country Trail Association, on behalf of the National Park Service — nct_public FeatureServer, layer 2, “North Country Trail – Public”, pull of November 21, 2026. Drawn in three classes: solid green for natural tread (built off-road trail), solid amber for urban sidewalk, dashed red for road.

Interpretation (the three-class split). The urban-sidewalk split is derived from the source data by a documented rule, not published by NCTA: a mile counts as urban sidewalk if NCTA itself tags the segment trail_type = ‘Sidewalk’ (28.90 miles trail-wide), or if an on-road mile falls inside a Census 2020 Urban Area or a Census 2024 incorporated place. Everything else on-road counts as road. The three styles are three different claims about the ground, and only the first comes straight from NCTA’s data as tread.

Chapters. Chapter extents — which chapter looks after which stretch — come from the NCTA’s chapter pages and the affiliate organisations’ own pages, read November 23–24, 2026; the full per-chapter table, with sources, is in the repository’s ncta-chapter-extents-2026-11-23.md. Per-chapter mileage is the sum of the source data’s miles field for the features tagged with that chapter’s code, from the same November 21, 2026 pull. Chapter mileage is attribution, not a survey: 152.76 miles carry no chapter code at all (listed as Unassigned, mostly in North Dakota), and a few codes are variants or unidentified (2.46 miles tagged “Central NY”, a variant spelling of Central New York; 1.56 miles tagged GTR; 1.48 miles tagged LON on the New Rockford Canal). Where a variant is reasonably identified it is folded into its chapter’s highlight and noted on the chapter’s record; where it is not, it sits in Unassigned and is flagged, not hidden. One chapter is short in the data: Star of the North shows only 10.21 tagged miles though the chapter maintains about 27 — its remaining miles are probably tagged under other codes or Unassigned. The NCTA publishes no per-chapter mileage; its 2025 Trail Progress Report gives state totals only, and the state totals from the same pull agree with the NCTA’s 2025 figures within a few miles per state (closest: Minnesota, +0.15 miles; largest gaps: Ohio +6.78, Michigan −5.08), which the NCTA’s own caveat covers: totals are estimates and change frequently.

Trail alerts — NCTA (83 points)

Source: NCTA trail_alerts FeatureServer, layer 1 “trail_alerts”. Pulled 2026-11-23 by scripts/pull_trail_alerts_national.py. File: data/trail-alerts.geojson.

What. NCTA’s own current-awareness notices — closures, storm damage, reroutes, new routes — quoted verbatim, credited to NCTA. 83 alert points, dates 2020-12-31 to 2026-11-15 (the service keeps old alerts; nothing here is filtered by date). Two alerts share a point and a date (East Fork State Park, 2026-08-20; Garrison Dam, 2026-09-10) — they are distinct service records, kept as returned, not deduplicated.

Interpretation. One national envelope over the trail extent (lon −102.0..−72.3, lat 38.2..48.6, widened slightly past the centerline so no alert on the trail itself is clipped). Features fetched by object id in chunk-verified chunks of 500 (offset paging on these hosts has silently truncated before). Service count cross-checked at pull time: returnCountOnly = 83, fetched 83. Positions come solely from the returned GeoJSON geometry (outSR=4326). Title, location, and description are carried verbatim — alerts are quoted, never rewritten or summarised.

Date handling — read before “fixing”. The service stores each alert’s Date as UTC midnight of the day AFTER the intended calendar date (a date-only field authored in a US timezone). The intended date is the America/Chicago calendar date of the stored instant: 1753574400000 is stored as 2025-07-27T00:00Z and means 2025-07-26. The file therefore carries BOTH the raw epoch milliseconds (date_ms, verbatim) and the derived ISO calendar date (date, the America/Chicago date of date_ms). The map displays date; never re-derive it as the UTC date.

Bridges — crossing opportunities (FHWA National Bridge Inventory 2025; 1,415 structures)

Source: FHWA National Bridge Inventory 2025, all-records delimited file, 2025allstatesallrecsdel.zip (58 MB). Pulled 2026-11-23 by scripts/pull_nbi_bridges_national.py. Code values decoded per the FHWA Recording and Coding Guide FHWA-PD-96-001. Raw download cached at working/bridges/2025allstatesallrecsdel.zip. File: data/nbi-bridges.geojson.

What. Existing highway bridges that might carry the trail — crossing opportunities. Two tiers: Tier 1, 372 (355 Highway-pedestrian + 17 Pedestrian-bicycle); Tier 2, 1,043 (Highway within 400 m). By state: Ohio 588, New York 250, Michigan 186, Minnesota 185, Pennsylvania 87, North Dakota 85, Wisconsin 24, Vermont 10, New Hampshire 0, Maine 0. Status: 1,281 open, 119 posted for load, 11 posted for other load-capacity restriction, 2 not applicable, 1 open with temporary shoring, 1 open temporary structure; 0 closed.

Interpretation. Only the ten states the NCT crosses are extracted (149,615 records → 122,106 distinct structures). New Hampshire and Maine contribute zero kept structures: the centerline has no trail miles in either state — the NCT’s eastern terminus is in Vermont. The all-records file carries multiple records per structure (one per inventory route on/under it); dedupe is keyed by (state, structure number) — the state is part of the key because structure numbers repeat across states — keeping record type 1 (route on the structure) where one exists, else the first record; nbi_records carries the merged record count. Coordinates: NBI LAT_016 / LONG_017 are DDMMSS.ss (e.g. 45011920 = 45° 01′ 19.20″ N); converted to decimal degrees here — the field is fixed-width with exactly two decimal digits on the seconds (an earlier draft parsed the seconds as a plain float and shifted every point up to ~0.8°; this is the fix). 118 structures were dropped for unparseable or out-of-range coordinates. Corridor is the 2-mile buffer around the centerline. The national line spans UTM zones 14–19, so no single UTM zone is used: a kilometre equirectangular STRtree pre-filter (44° N, generous radii — 2.2 mi for Tier 1, 450 m for Tier 2) selects candidates, and the published dist_trail_km is then measured exactly in a local tangent plane centred on each bridge. Tier 1: service on in {3 Pedestrian-bicycle, 5 Highway-pedestrian}, within 2 mi. [JUDGMENT] Tier 2: service on = 1 Highway, within 0.25 mi (400 m) — a vehicular bridge the trail route actually passes is one a hiker could use as a crossing without a detour; a 2-mi vehicular net would be every county-road bridge in the corridor and unreadable as a crossing-opportunities layer. Excluded in both tiers: K = bridge closed to all traffic, G = new structure not yet open. Blank open/closed codes are KEPT: the field does not apply to non-highway records, so a blank is not a closed bridge. Not carried: service on 2 Railroad, 4 Highway-railroad, 6/7/8 interchange levels, 9 Building/plaza, 0 Other. 848 structures dropped on status, 2,842 on service, 116,883 on distance. County names are decoded from the U.S. Census TIGERweb State_County service, not from a hand-kept table.

Source substitution. The intended source was the MnDOT bridge inventory trans-bridges (Minnesota Geospatial Commons; audited in the ITM repo, verdict: Use). It could NOT be pulled on 2026-11-18: the MnGeo enterprise host returns 499 Token Required; the GDRS download paths all return 404; MnDOT’s own host is unreachable from this VM; no ArcGIS Online copy was found; the DNR Bridges Cartegraph service also 404s — apparently retired/moved. NBI is the federal inventory fed by the states from their own inspection systems, so the structures and bridge numbers are the same inventory — but it is NOT the MnDOT service. Follow-up: re-pull from trans-bridges when MnDOT access is sorted (live browser or token); the bridge number is the join key. The all-records file is used, not the per-state highway-only files, because pedestrian-only bridges (service on = 3) are non-highway records and are absent from the highway files.

Wetlands (NWI) — classified for trail routing (352,865 polygons)

Source: USFWS National Wetlands Inventory, national service as republished on ArcGIS Online, USA_Wetlands FeatureServer layer 0 (maxRecordCount 1000). Pulled 2026-11-23 by scripts/pull_nwi_corridor_national.py (fetch log working/nwi/pull-run-2026-11-23.log; raw fetch working/nwi/raw-nwi.geojson, 377,906 unique features, 205 MB). File: data/nwi-wetlands.geojson, 174 MB — OFF by default, loads only when switched on. Style (as ITM): MAYBE amber fill (#f59e0b, 0.35) with a dashed edge, NO-GO solid red fill (#dc2626, 0.45); dissolved features ordered MAYBE then NO-GO so NO-GO draws on top where they overlap.

What. The NWI along the full trail, classified GO / MAYBE / NO-GO for trail routing. 352,865 polygons, 1,453,362.8 ac, in the 2-mile corridor around the full centerline; a wetland counts when its polygon intersects the buffer. By rating: MAYBE 309,439 / NO-GO 43,426. There are NO GO features in the file: GO is absence of a polygon (rule W1). By rule: W6 99,842, W6-analog 58,586, W7 56,734, W8 51,797, W9 21,722, W3 19,750, W5 17,534, W4 15,453, W9-analog 11,440, UNCLASSIFIED 7. By state: MN 107,313; WI 61,320; ND 48,711; MI 45,127; OH 41,919; NY 37,168; VT 5,715; PA 5,328; state null 264 (centroids outside the state polygons — border waters).

Service substitutions (verified live). The brief’s URL (fws.gov/wetlands/arcgis/rest/services/Wetlands/MapServer) returns 404 — the service moved to ArcGIS Online (the URL above). The DNR’s own updated-NWI service returns 499 Token Required — not publicly queryable. A research report’s R4b citation (Wetlands_RLIS FeatureServer) is WRONG — it is Portland Metro RLIS (Oregon State Plane North) and returns 0 features on the trail. Do not use it. inSR=4326 is REQUIRED on the USA_Wetlands service — envelope queries without it fail with 400.

Pull method. The corridor is cut into 0.25° cells (4,485 cells in the envelope, 328 intersecting the corridor); each cell is queried by geometry POST (the cell’s intersection with the corridor buffer, simplified 0.002°, widened 0.5 km — a superset; the exact clip happens in Python), rings in ArcGIS JSON format, resultOffset pagination 1,000/page ordered by OBJECTID, with a returnCountOnly cross-check per cell. Dedupe by OBJECTID across cells: 377,906 unique features. Clip to the exact corridor in Python: 335,738 polygons kept; 42,111 fell outside the corridor (the query geometry was a widened superset), 57 invalid geometries skipped, 0 topology failures. 12,868 source codes failed parse_code() and were classified from the service’s parsed fields instead (the classify fallback — counted, not dropped). Dissolve: touching polygons with the same (rating, Cowardin code) merged into connected components, exploded — 352,865 output features (more than the 335,738 clipped inputs, because a union can split into several components). Simplification: server-side maxAllowableOffset 0.0002 (~22 m) + geometryPrecision 5; coordinates rounded to 4 decimals. Memory note (differs from ITM): ITM built the corridor as one unary_union + buffer of its 348-line Minnesota line. The national line has 4,756 pieces; a whole-line union + buffer was OOM-killed on this VM once before, so this script NEVER builds one corridor polygon — it buffers each line piece individually, indexes the buffers in an STRtree, and unions only the few pieces near each cell / polygon. The corridor test is identical (intersects the 2-mi buffer); only the construction is chunked.

Classification — the interpretation method (Nov 18, 2026 classification sweep; full text in ITM SOURCES.md, “Wetlands (NWI)” — this script implements it, it does not re-derive it). NO published Cowardin→GO/MAYBE/NO-GO matrix exists — this classification is a flagged synthesis, and every judgment stays labeled as judgment. Apply in order; first rule that fires wins:

  • W1 — No polygon → GO (screening only). Judgment J1: GO as absence is the chapter’s definition.
  • W2 — Overlay: overlaps DNR public waters, a calcareous fen, or a Minnesota Biological Survey rare natural community → NO-GO pending agency advice, regardless of code. NOT APPLIED in this build — NWI alone cannot identify these and the viewer has no public-waters / Natural Heritage layer yet (open gap).
  • W3 — Open / permanent water: regime H, Lacustrine UB, or PUB/PAB with H → NO-GO. Judgment J2: the categorical H→NO-GO is judgment.
  • W4 — Large bog / muskeg complex: PFO2/PFO4/PSS2/3/4 with regime B/C → NO-GO for a through-route; MAYBE only for a short, named, maintainable spur. Judgment J3: the spur exception and the word “large” (no numeric threshold is set). Build note: no size gate is applied — every bog-class B/C complex is NO-GO for a through-route, because no published threshold exists; the size question is left to the field/route check.
  • W5 — Semipermanently flooded (regime F) → MAYBE, short narrow boardwalk only; >~300 ft of F-regime tread = NO-GO. Judgment J4: the 300-ft threshold; the length check is route-level, not applied in the layer.
  • W6 — Seasonally flooded/saturated (regime C/E) → MAYBE, bog bridge / boardwalk, short crossing only.
  • W7 — Temporarily flooded / saturated (regime A/B) → MAYBE, turnpike / puncheon. Judgment J5: rod-sounding field upgrade — if a rod meets no resistance, upgrade to NO-GO.
  • W8 — Riverine FIRST (before W3–W7): R2/R3 perennial → NO-GO along the channel (a single bridge point is a separate project decision); R4/R5 intermittent → MAYBE, bridge/boardwalk at a narrow point. Stay ≥25 ft from the stream edge except at the crossing.
  • W9 — Modifiers d (partly drained/ditched), f (farmed), x (excavated), or Class US / shore → MAYBE, field-check first; do NOT downgrade to GO. Judgment J6: the MAYBE placement.
  • W10 — Cumulative length through any MAYBE class on a proposed route >~½ mile → NO-GO for the route as drawn. Judgment J7: the ½-mile figure; route-level, not applied in the layer.
  • Combined rules C1–C4 (applied where the two layers are read together): C1 stricter governs — a NO-GO from either dataset is a NO-GO overall. C2 soil cannot rescue a wetland — a Not-limited soil inside any NWI polygon is never GO. C3 NWI polygon + hydric major component = highest-confidence wetland; hydric soil with no NWI polygon = MAYBE/field-check, never GO; NWI polygon on a non-hydric unit still governs. C4 absence from BOTH datasets is the only GO (plus a side-hill 3–10% alignment, rule S4) — screening only.
  • Build-time judgments (extensions of the report’s register, labeled here): J13 — regime D (Continuously Saturated) is not named in the report’s rules; treated as W6-analog MAYBE (bog bridge/boardwalk), the same treatment as C/E. J14 — regime K (Artificially Flooded) is not named in the report’s rules; treated as W9-analog MAYBE (field-check; water level is managed). Both are the chapter’s judgment, not a published rule. The parser also handles split classes (e.g. PFO1/4D) by reading the regime from the END of the full code, and regime-less codes (e.g. PUS) by parsing against the known class set.

What each feature carries. code (Cowardin), wetland_type, system, class, subclass, regime, regime_name, modifier, rating (maybe | nogo), rule (short id: W3–W9, W6-analog, W9-analog, UNCLASSIFIED), acres, n_polygons (source polygons merged into this feature), state (from site/data/states.geojson, by centroid; null where the centroid falls outside those polygons, e.g. border waters). The full rule text (structure + note + judgment tag) is NOT repeated per feature — it lives once in working/nwi/wetland-rules.json (and in the pull script’s RULE_TEXT table, which the shell’s app.js WETLAND_RULES mirrors). State replaces ITM’s county/region, which were Minnesota-specific.

Dataset limits. Minimum mapping unit 0.5 ac; identification ~90%, classification ~75%; 95% of features within ±5.9 m. Intended use 1:12,000 or smaller. NWI is a screening layer, not a delineation: field delineation + permits before any MAYBE becomes a route.

Regulatory gates. Federal Clean Water Act §404, Nationwide Permit 42 (2026 reissue, ≤½ acre, pre-construction notification required). Minnesota Wetland Conservation Act: avoid → minimize → replace; de minimis exemption 400 sq ft / 10,000 sq ft, type- and location-dependent — confirm with the local government unit. (Other states have their own wetland programs; the Minnesota gates are stated here because the classification sweep was written for Minnesota — do not read them as national law.)

Open gaps. (1) W2 overlay not applied (above). (2) W4 has no numeric “large” threshold (above). (3) W5 300-ft and W10 ½-mile are route-level checks — they need an actual drawn route, not a layer. (4) Per-polygon vintage is not exposed by the service. (Minnesota’s content in the national file is the DNR June 2019 update delivered to FWS; other states’ vintages vary and are not stated by the service.)

Tribal lands — BIA AIAN Land Area Representation (11 reservations)

Source: Bureau of Indian Affairs, American Indian and Alaska Native Land Area Representation (AIAN-LAR), BIA_AIAN_National_LAR FeatureServer, layer 0. Pulled 2026-11-23 by scripts/pull_tribal_lands_national.py (adapted from ITM’s per-reservation pulls). Public federal data; no licence restriction. File: data/tribal-lands.geojson.

What. Every reservation whose boundary comes within 2 miles of the NCT — 11 reservations (full unclipped geometry): Bad River (LAR0015, 0.08 trail mi inside), Grand Portage (LAR0022, 0.0 mi inside, 2.31 km from the trail), Huron Potawatomi (LAR0026, 0.0 mi, 0.60 km), L’Anse (LAR0029, 0.0 mi, 3.18 km), Leech Lake (LAR0033, 12.01 mi inside), Little River (LAR0034, 0.45 mi), Little Traverse Bay (LAR0035, 0.0 mi, 0.23 km), Sault Ste. Marie (LAR0046, 0.0 mi, 1.03 km), White Earth (LAR0051, 18.95 mi inside), Allegany (LAR0297, 6.76 mi inside), Oneida, New York (LAR0319, 33.49 mi inside).

Interpretation. The whole layer (335 features) is fetched by object id in chunk-verified chunks of 100; a reservation is IN when its polygon intersects the 2-mile corridor around the centerline. Distance is tested in a kilometre equirectangular projection against per-piece 2-mi buffers indexed in an STRtree. The closest call is L’Anse at 3.18 km against a 3.21869 km threshold — the projection overstates lon distances at 46.7° N, so the true distance is slightly less; it is inside on either measure. Per-reservation trail miles inside are measured by intersecting the centerline with the reservation polygon and summing geodesic lengths (pyproj Geod, WGS84) of the inside pieces. Cross-checks against ITM’s Minnesota measurements: White Earth 18.95 mi (ITM: 19 mi — agrees); Leech Lake 12.01 mi vs ITM’s 2 mi — NOT a disagreement: ITM measured only its chapter’s depicted chain (Chip-12’s 2 mi); the full line has 12.01 mi inside, in 3 contiguous segments around lon −94.28..−94.11, lat 47.00..47.05 (east of Walker, outside the chapter extent). Oneida (New York) 33.49 mi in 25 segments — the NCT/Finger Lakes Trail weaving through the Oneida boundary south of Syracuse; the BIA polygon there is the large historic boundary.

What is NOT used, per the ITM rule. BIA’s own GISACRES attribute contradicts its own geometry (Leech Lake: 1,794,106 vs 823,249 ac geometry-measured; White Earth: 1,621,000.16 vs 748,098 ac — both ~2.17x), so area_acres here is geometry-measured (geodesic, WGS84) and GISACRES is carried only as gisacres_attribute for the record. The Census off-reservation trust-land features are NOT included: this layer is reservations themselves, as in ITM. Neither source came from the tribe itself: both are federal publications. The popup and this page carry the line: the federal reservation boundary as published — not a legal survey.

Public land (PAD-US) — live service, no local file

Source: USGS Protected Areas Database of the United States (PAD-US) 4.1 (March 2025), DOI 10.5066/P96WBCHS. Consumed LIVE from the ArcGIS service; no local PAD-US file is downloaded. Services verified live 2026-11-22 and re-checked 2026-11-23. Public services, no key required. See site/data/padus-note.md and site/data/padus-layer.json for the wiring. The viewer renders the combined-inventory raster (server-rendered) with a clickable Manager Name vector for popups; Esri Leaflet is vendored at site/esri-leaflet.js (v3.0.12). The raster uses direct image export (f: "image") rather than the default JSON round-trip: the JSON path returns a rendered-PNG href on the server's arcgisoutput host, which does not answer from the test environment, so nothing draws; the direct path loads the PNG straight from the MapServer and renders (verified in-browser 2026-11-23).

Services. Combined inventory (fee + designations + easements + marine + proclamation boundaries), layer 0 PADUS4_1Combined: PAD_US_gaz_combined/MapServer. Fee only (fee-simple footprint, no designations/easements), layer 0 PADUS4_1Fee: PAD_US_4_1/MapServer. Manager Name feature service (clickable, same PAD-US 4.1 data): Manager_Name_PADUS/FeatureServer, layer 0. The cached tile service PADUS3_0PublicAccess is PAD-US 3.0 (2022 vintage) and tiles-only (no attributes) — do not use as primary.

Interpretation — why live, not local. A corridor pull is too heavy for a local file: PAD_US_gaz_combined layer 0 returned 192 features for one small 0.4° × 0.3° envelope near Duluth, MN, and 1,016 features for a same-size envelope near Columbus, OH (count-only queries, 2026-11-22). The trail corridor ribbon (~2 mi buffer around 4,871 mi of trail, ~19,500 sq mi) would yield thousands of features, including very large complex polygons (national-forest proclamation boundaries with tens of thousands of vertices), paginated at 2,000 per page, each then needing a local clip to the ribbon and simplification. The whole-line corridor buffer itself could not be built in this VM in one shapely pass (the unary_union + 2 mi buffer of 4,373 lines was OOM-killed); a later local pull should build the buffer in chunks (e.g. per-state with ogr2ogr). If a local pull is done later: query PAD_US_gaz_combined/0 with geometry = the 2 mi corridor buffer as Esri JSON, geometryType=esriGeometryPolygon, spatialRel=esriSpatialRelIntersects, outSR=4326, paginated with resultOffset (maxRecordCount 2000). Run a returnCountOnly query first to size it. Clip each returned polygon to the buffer locally, simplify, and keep only FeatClass, Category, Own_Type, Own_Name, Mang_Type, Mang_Name, Unit_Nm, State_Nm, GIS_Acres, Pub_Access, GAP_Sts.

Caveats. PAD-US does not tag which easements serve the NCT; any trail-easement reading needs an intersection with the trail corridor, and many trail easements (NCTA, local land trusts, handshake agreements being converted) may not be in it at all. The Easement class is NCED-derived (non-sensitive easements only); Date_Est is ‘not fully attributed’. USGS strongly discourages comparing two PAD-US versions to look for changes in protection status. Combined-layer records overlap by design (fee + designation + easement on the same ground); the Fee layer is the non-overlapping footprint. Local park data gaps exist below the state level.

Parcels — county records (141,687 parcels, 3,031.45 trail miles)

Built 2026-11-24 by scripts/pull_parcels_state.py (one pull per state) + scripts/merge_parcels.py (global corridor selection). Option A from parcels-scope-2026-11-23.md — statewide services only. File: data/parcels.geojson (103,329,590 bytes; 141,687 features; 15,596 crossed + 126,091 adjoining). OFF by default, lazy-loaded on first toggle; faint below zoom 11, full style at zoom 11+; crossed parcels 2px solid orange outline, adjoining 2px dashed khaki (the ITM model).

Sources (all verified live at pull time, 2026-11-24). ND GIS Hub NDGISHUB_Parcels FeatureServer layer 0; MnGeo plan_parcels_open FeatureServer layer 0 (quarterly snapshot, live rundate 2026-11-09; per-county acqdate Jul–Nov 2026, in working/parcels/vintage-MN.json); Wisconsin Statewide Parcel Map Database V12 FeatureServer layer 0 (released 2026-06-30, tax roll mostly 2025); VCGI Parcel Program FS_VCGI_VTPARCELS_WM_NOCACHE_v2 FeatureServer layer 1 (weekly updates, 2025 grand list); NYS ITS NYS_Tax_Parcels_Public FeatureServer layer 1 (2025 data, published May 2026, annual); OGRIP/ODNR Statewide_Parcels_ODNR_WebMerc FeatureServer layer 10 (2023-vintage snapshot, per-parcel currentto Jan 2023–Mar 2024; no refresh cadence stated). Full evidence base: parcels-scope-2026-11-23.md and ~/workspace/nct-parcels-scope/state-*.md.

Pull method. Candidates are parcels intersecting the 2.5 km band around the centerline, queried per 0.25° cell whose slice of the band is non-empty (the NWI pattern: cell ∩ corridor buffer, simplified, widened 0.5 km — a superset; the exact selection happens in Python). Per cell, returnIdsOnly first, then fetch by object-id chunks of 400 with per-chunk count verification (offset-paged envelope queries silently truncate on the MnGeo host, so fetching is by id — the ITM pattern). County filter where the service carries a county field (MN co_name, ND CountyName, WI CONAME, NY COUNTY_NAME, OH county); VT has no county field, so the whole corridor band is queried and the county is derived from TIGER in the merge. 612,923 candidates pulled (ND 15,262; MN 141,371; WI 11,728; VT 5,752; NY 48,457; OH 390,353). Resume is per-county with a checkpoint file; a kill mid-county used to duplicate that county on re-run (found on the NY/WI/MN/OH pulls) — the script now dedupes on resume by src_oid, and the merge dedupes again.

Interpretation — corridor selection (the ITM model, applied globally across all six states). Crossed: a parcel the NCT line crosses or touches, or whose boundary comes within 1 m of the line (tolerance absorbs digitising offsets between datasets). Adjoining: a remaining candidate whose boundary comes within 3 m of a crossed parcel’s boundary — one ring deep. Selection runs in EPSG:5070 on the full geometry; output geometry is simplified 2 m, topology-preserving, coordinates rounded to 6 decimals. Each feature carries only the ITM display fields — parcel ID, county, state, land class, acres, owner name, mailing address as published — plus source, vintage and corridor class. Taxpayer fields (assessed values, taxes, EMV) were never requested from any service and are not in the file. Land class is the source’s own class, labelled where the code needs it: NY ORPTS series (1 Agricultural … 9 Wild/forested/conservation) + code, WI property class (1 Residential … 7 Other) + code; ND carries the service’s Ownership value (often null); OH has no class field, so land class is null there. Owner names ARE shown, as published in the county records (public record — the chapter ruling of Nov 10, 2026, carried to the national layer). Cross-checks run when the layers were wired in (2026-11-23)

Two independent builds derived chapter boundaries and chainage from the same centerline by different methods. They were cross-checked before wiring, not silently merged.

Chapter boundaries: step 3 (86 junctions + 2 termini) vs gazetteer (38 named boundaries)

Coordinates agree. 37 of the gazetteer’s 38 boundaries match a step-3 junction with the same chapter pair: the maximum distance between matched points is 0.002 mi (essentially the same point — both come from the centerline). The one gazetteer boundary with no step-3 counterpart is FLY → SRV at chainage 258.34: in the step-3 chain the two chapters never touch directly — 114 mi of chapter-unassigned (None-coded) centerline runs between them (FLY → None at 95.77 mi, None → SRV at 262.11 mi), and the gazetteer names the junction across that unassigned stretch from the extents table, which step 3 does not do.

The other 49 step-3 junctions are real data junctions, not errors. They are the interleavings of unassigned and variant codes with the named chapters: None in/out pairs (FLY, BSC, PWC, MAC, BTA, CLA, ANF, CNY, MALT), the GTR variant on GTC ground (8 junctions), the “Central NY” variant spelling (6 junctions), the 0.01 mi LLC sliver inside ITM ground (2 junctions), and the CLA/BUT reversal (2 junctions, the two chapters interleave). The gazetteer collapses these into one named boundary per chapter pair; step 3 shows every chapter change in the data. Disposition: the map layer is the step-3 file (86 junctions + 2 termini, exact centerline positions); the gazetteer search uses its own 38 named boundaries. Neither set was altered to match the other.

Chainages differ systematically — that is expected, not an error. For the 37 matched pairs the chainage difference runs 0.66–9.06 mi (median 2.31 mi), with the sign flipping by stretch. The two chains are built differently: step 3 accumulates the source miles along a deduplicated walk (duplicates and leftovers removed, gap jumps add 0 mi); the gazetteer accumulates stored segment miles plus the straight-line miles of every gap its walk jumped (26.05 mi). Where step 3 removed many duplicate miles the step-3 chainage runs lower; where the gazetteer added gap miles it runs higher. The two totals differ by 1.20 mi for the same reason (below). A reader comparing a boundary’s chainage between the map popup and a gazetteer search result should expect a difference of this size.

Chainage totals: step 3 (4,826.21 mi) vs gazetteer chain walk (4,827.41 mi) — 1.20 mi apart

The cause is determined, not guessed. Both start from the same raw miles sum, 4,871.07 mi. Step 3: 4,871.07 − 9.68 mi exact duplicates − 35.18 mi leftovers (alternative alignments / spurs / overlapping duplicates) = 4,826.21 mi. The gazetteer: 4,871.07 − 69.71 mi unchained segments + 26.05 mi straight-line gap miles = 4,827.41 mi. Net exclusions: step 3 removes 44.86 mi; the gazetteer removes 43.66 mi net (69.71 − 26.05). The 1.20 mi delta is exactly that 44.86 − 43.66 difference. In words: the gazetteer’s walk leaves 69.71 mi unchained (more than step 3’s 35.18 mi of leftovers, because its 150 m join tolerance strands more short stubs and parallel alternates) but then adds back 26.05 mi of straight-line gap distance that step 3 counts as 0. Disposition: the map’s chainage layer uses step 3’s chainage.geojson (4,826.21 mi); gazetteer search results carry the gazetteer’s chainage (4,827.41 mi total). Both numbers are shown where they are used, and neither is presented as the only total.

Coverage gaps — stated, not filled

  • Soils: no soils layer yet (service recovered Nov 23; national pull not run). The wetlands C2–C4 combined rules that read soils together with NWI therefore cannot be applied in the viewer.
  • Mile markers: no half-mile index for Minnesota east of mile 558.5 (~317 mi, Border Route / Superior Hiking Trail stretches), the ~932 mi Buckeye Trail portion of Ohio, or the ~390 mi Finger Lakes Trail portion of New York (~430 mi unindexed).
  • NCTA map sheets: Ohio 13%, New York 40%, Minnesota 67% of centerline miles inside a published sheet.
  • Wetlands: W2 overlay not applied; no numeric “large” threshold for W4; W5/W10 are route-level checks; per-polygon vintage not exposed.
  • Partner trails: SHT ~100 mi missing from the DNR park file (southern terminus approach through Jay Cooke State Park); BRT 46.07 mi vs ~65 mi published; Driftless Weaver Road missing (not in OSM); Long Trail OSM 249.50 mi vs 272 mi published.

  • Parcels: 63.7% of trail miles covered (3,031.45 mi); Michigan entirely blank, NY’s 5 opt-out counties blank, Pennsylvania mostly blank, Hubbard County MN blank, Mercer County ND blank (source has no parcels touching the trail there). Backfill would add 564.61 mi (75.6%). Ohio’s statewide service is a 2023-vintage snapshot.
