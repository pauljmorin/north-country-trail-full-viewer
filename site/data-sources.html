Data sources

North Country Trail completeness viewer — every layer carries its references and its interpretation method: the exact rules used to turn raw data into the published classification, which attributes drove each call, and which placements are judgment. Someone reading this page later must be able to reproduce every classification from the references plus the stated rules.

A note on freshness. Most of these layers are snapshots, pulled on the dates given. A centerline pull is a record of its date, not a live feed — the pull date on each layer is the freshness signal. No one at NCTA or NPS was contacted for this viewer; everything here comes from public sources already on disk or cited below.

The centerline — three kinds of miles

Natural tread, urban sidewalk, road

The map draws the official route in three styles: solid green for natural tread (built off-road trail), solid amber for urban sidewalk, dashed red for road (trail not yet built). The three styles are three different claims, and only the first two come straight from NCTA’s data.

Source: North Country Trail Association, on behalf of the National Park Service — nct_public FeatureServer, layer 2, “North Country Trail – Public”. Layer description: “Existing Off-Road and On-Road route centerline segments of the North Country National Scenic Trail (NOCO), GCS North American 1983. Data is GPSd, digitized, or sourced from third-party data sets. Not for use at scales greater than 1:24,000.” 4,102 features, last edited 2026-11-01T12:54:37Z, pulled November 21, 2026 (paginated, 1,000/page, outSR=4326, f=geojson).

Plus the Superior Hiking Trail layer, same server — agol_sht_public FeatureServer, layer 1. 272 features, all Minnesota, chapter SHTA, 271 off-road + 1 on-road (0.10 mi). Pulled November 21, 2026. This is part of the official route and is NOT inside nct_public — without it Minnesota comes out ~300 miles short. Merged total: 4,870.55 len_miles / 4,871.24 geodesic miles. Overlap check: 0.13 mi of SHT lies within ~50 m of an nct_public line (0.0%) — no double-counting.

The urban rule — DERIVED, not NCTA data

NCTA’s centerline only tags on-road vs off-road (trail_stat: “NCT” vs “NCT (on-road)”). The urban-sidewalk / road split is derived, never presented as coming from NCTA. The rule (v1):

A mile counts as URBAN SIDEWALK if (a) NCTA itself tags the segment trail_type = 'Sidewalk' (69 features, 28.90 miles trail-wide, all under trail_stat NCT), or (b) an on-road mile falls inside a Census 2020 Urban Area or a Census 2024 incorporated place. Everything else on-road counts as ROAD.

Why not Urban Area only? In Minnesota the UA rule catches 0.62 miles — it misses essentially every small-town traverse (Fergus Falls, Vergas, Tower, Grand Rapids center), because most NCT towns are below the 2,000-person UA threshold. Why not place only? The place rule is the main driver (85.80 of 95.77 on-road urban miles trail-wide), but it has a known overcount — see the Cohasset flag below. Trail-wide the adopted rule gives 95.77 on-road urban miles (UA-only 24.92; incorporated-place-only 85.80; any-place including CDPs 101.89). Adding the 28.90 native-Sidewalk miles gives 124.68 urban sidewalk miles total.

Urban Areas source: Census 2020 Urban Areas, TIGERweb MapServer layer 1 (“2020 Urban Areas”, Jan 1, 2020 vintage), 2,645 polygons, pulled November 21, 2026. 13 polygons repaired with buffer(0); re-running after repair changed the trail-wide UA-or-place figure by +0.05 mi — immaterial, documented not hidden. Places source: Census 2024 cartographic boundary file cb_2024_us_place_500k (Mar 13, 2025), 32,612 features; incorporated = LSAD 21 borough / 25 city / 43 town / 47 village (19,696 features); CDPs are LSAD 57 and are excluded from the adopted rule (they would add 16.09 mi trail-wide; Vermont’s urban miles come from the Middlebury UA plus CDPs — Vermont has 0.00 incorporated-place miles).

Lengths — geodesic miles on WGS84

Every length is a geodesic length (WGS84, pyproj Geod) computed from each feature’s geometry — never taken from a precomputed field without a cross-check. Cross-checks: the len_miles field sums to 4,870.55 mi (0.69 mi / 0.01% below the geodesic total; median per-feature difference 0.02%, max 0.05%). Shape__Length (planar metres in UTM zone 16N) sums to 4,888.01 mi — 17 mi high, the expected UTM distortion away from zone 16; not used.

State assignment

Every segment was split at Census 2024 state boundaries (Shapely intersection, with overlap de-duplication), so state totals do not depend on NCTA’s native state field. The native field agrees almost everywhere (single-state mismatch 0.74 mi total) and was used only to assign the 9.92 mi that falls in no state polygon and the 21 features with state = null (8.70 mi MN, 37.42 mi OH, 4.75 mi PA — all on-road, recovered geometrically). 11 features cross a state line.

State boundaries: Census 2024 cartographic boundary file cb_2024_us_state_500k (Mar 13, 2025).

Chapter rollup

Native chapter field; no chapter-boundary geometry exists yet, and the ncta_region field is NULL on all 4,102 nct_public features, so there is no native region rollup. Chapter totals sum to the trail total; the gaps affect per-chapter attribution, not the state table. See the flags section below for the unmapped codes.

What was checked and excluded (not part of the existing route)

  • agol_flt (Finger Lakes Trail, 417 features, 382.46 mi) — 100.0% of sampled points lie within ~50 m of an nct_public line. Duplicate publication of the FLTC miles already in nct_public; excluded to avoid double-counting.
  • agol_generalized_vermont_extension (20 features, 41.77 mi) — generalized duplicate of part of the Vermont route; excluded.
  • adirondack__route (290 features, 226.94 mi) — the service itself says “For Planning purposes only… proposed North Country” route through Adirondack Park. Not existing trail; excluded from the mileage table. It is the largest known planning gap and belongs in the viewer as a separate “proposed” layer, not in these totals.
  • nct_hss (3 features, 0 mi), Old_Route_of_NCT, border_plan_rd_walk_agol, Potential_County_Road_Walk, MI_GTR_gl2kal (proposed Guernsey Lake–Kalkaska reroute) — planning/old-route data, excluded.

State boundaries (click a state for its rollup)

Thin grey boundaries from the same Census 2024 state file used for the state assignment above. The popup rollup is the same three-way mileage as the state table on the map page.

PAD-US public lands

The ownership backbone under the gaps: the USGS Protected Areas Database of the United States (PAD-US), the national public-lands dataset, one pull. Off by default; it answers “who owns the ground” where the trail isn’t built yet.

Status, November 22, 2026: not yet wired. The data agent’s note (site/data/padus-note.md) has not landed yet, so the viewer shows PAD-US as a clearly-marked placeholder (“pending — no data yet”) in the layer panel, and switching it on does nothing. The note will state whether PAD-US arrives as a GeoJSON file or a live tile service, in type: / file: / url: lines, and the viewer wires it from that note — GeoJSON file, or tile layer with the note’s attribution. Nothing is guessed in the meantime.

The case panel (case.html)

Build rate, gap closure, finish scenarios

Build-rate research, November 2026: miles built per year 2015–2025 from NCTA annual and trail-progress reports, the North Star magazine, and IRS Form 990s; roadwalk eliminated per year 2020–2025 from the same reports; net GIS on-road decline 2019–2025 from the annual reports’ own off-road/on-road totals. Scenarios A–H divide the 1,495-mile end-2025 on-road gap by the stated rates. 2017 miles built could not be verified (no report or 990 figure located; ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer blocked in the research environment). No source publishes miles built by builder type, and no current NPS-certified total is published (latest is 2018, “over 1,775 miles”).

Sources (full URLs in the build-rate report, ~/workspace/research_notes/nct-build-rate-20261121-2212/report.md): NCTA 2025 Annual Report; NCTA Form 990s 2023 and 2025; NCTA 2025–2030 Strategic Plan; NCTA 2020–2023 Strategic Plan Priority Objectives; NCTA Trail Progress Reports 2016, 2019, 2020; NCTA 2018 Annual Report; North Star, Winter 2022 (41-1), Winter 2023 (42-1), Winter 2024 (43-1), Winter 2025 (44-1); National Trails System Act (16 U.S.C. §1244); NPCA, “The Long Way”; NCTA FY2027 Funding + Legislative Requests and FY27 state fact sheets (Feb 2026).

What limits the rate

Land/easements (willing-seller-only authority, P.L. 111-11, 2009; no eminent domain, ever; <50 NPS-owned miles); funding (~$1.5M/yr NPS operations; NCTA revenue $2.01M/$2.15M/$1.81M in 2023/2024/2025); volunteer capacity (~1,000–1,300 volunteers/yr, 65,000–91,000 hours/yr, 15–17 paid staff); permitting (Adirondack moratorium, BWCA/NEPA, national-forest NEPA for reroutes/bridges/shelters). Each claim is cited to its source in the build-rate report.

Epoch history (1982 → 2005 → 2026)

Snapshot comparison, November 21–22, 2026, public sources only. Net line change between dated pictures of the route: +1,896 mi (1982→2005, 200 m tolerance) and +1,907 mi (2005→2026, 100 m tolerance); both nets stable to ±2% across tolerances. Gross “new” figures (2,436 and 2,694 mi) are upper bounds mixing construction, re-routes, re-digitization, and coverage change — not miles built.

Flags — shown, not hidden

Cohasset, MN — 7.59 mi. The largest single “urban” chunk in Minnesota is a highway road-walk inside Cohasset’s 35-square-mile city limits (pop. ~2,700). Most of it has no sidewalk; the place rule counts it as urban anyway. It is 58% of Minnesota’s 13.03 urban miles and the biggest known overcount in the table. A future refinement could screen by road class or sidewalk presence; no such dataset is in hand.

~131 miles tagged to no chapter. 130.92 mi carry chapter = the literal string “None” (mostly ND, 32 features; VT 6) and 21.84 mi carry null. Unmapped codes: ADK-ON 38.45 mi (NY, Finger Lakes Trail segments — probably an ADK data-entry variant), GTR 1.56 mi (MI, Guernsey Lake–Kalkaska proposed reroute area), LON 1.48 mi (ND, New Rockford Canal), plus “Central NY” 2.46 mi (variant of CNY). Chapter totals still sum to the trail total. Star of the North (STN) has only 10.21 mi tagged, though the chapter maintains ~27 mi — its remaining miles are probably tagged under other codes or “None”.

Minnesota ~8 mi off/on shift vs the 2025 Annual Report. Ours: 733.86 off / 141.37 on; report: 742 / 133. The totals match (+0.2) but ~8 mi sits on opposite sides of the off/on line. Prime suspect: the six state-null Arrowhead on-road segments near Grand Rapids (8.70 mi) — possibly counted as off-road in the report’s vintage, or reclassified between the report and the Nov 1, 2026 centerline. Open question; the numbers were not adjusted to match the report. (Michigan on-road: ours 266.20 vs report 274, −7.8 — small vintage drift, same direction.) Published “~4,800 miles” is a round public number; the annual report’s state table sums to 4,860; the measured total is 4,871.24.

cert_stat is not usable for the completeness claim. Values: Certified 1,510.92 mi, Connector 2,326.40 mi, null 1,001.14 mi, blank 17.99 mi, “Exisiting” [sic] 6.55 mi, lowercase “connector” 8.24 mi. “Connector” is the largest bucket and its meaning in NCTA’s data model is undocumented in the public layer. NPS’s older published figure (“1,775+ miles certified”) matches neither the Certified bucket nor Certified+Connector. Until NCTA clarifies, completeness rests on trail_stat only.

Small data defects. One feature (OBJECTID 138, Buckeye Trail on-road, OH/BTA) has no geometry and len_miles 0 — a zero-length placeholder, no effect on totals. trail_surf holds numeric junk on 8 features. trail_type is null/blank/Unknown on 298.71 off-road miles and 171.71 on-road miles. 9.92 mi of Michigan shoreline/bridge trail (Mackinac Bridge 4.09 mi, Lake Superior shore, Pictured Rocks) falls outside the 500k-generalized state polygon and was assigned to its native state (MI) — the generalization, not the trail, is wrong there.

What this viewer does not show

By design (scope, November 21, 2026): no photo pins or classification, no shoulder-width measurement, no speed badges, no ATV layer, no timber exams, no Cass County datasets, no census switcher, no historical maps, no 1939 aerials, no campsites / water access / trailheads as trip features, no “what’s walkable today” lead. Parcels along gaps only are a later step, not in v1.
